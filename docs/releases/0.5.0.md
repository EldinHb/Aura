## Upgrading from 0.4.0

Aura 0.4.0 and earlier cannot update themselves. **Install 0.5.0 by hand once**: download
the installer for your system below and install it over your current Aura, as you
installed it the first time. Your sessions, settings and mods are kept. From 0.5.0 on,
Aura updates itself.

## What's new

### Aura updates itself

- Shortly after it starts, and every 6 hours after that, Aura looks for a newer stable
  release, downloads it in the background and checks its signature. A banner then
  offers **Restart to update**, with the release notes and **Later**.
- Restarting stops the daemon, installs the new version and opens it. Your sessions are
  paused for that moment and resume afterwards; if a session is working, Aura asks first.
- Check by hand with ⌘K → Check for updates, `Ctrl+Space u` or the tray menu. Turn
  automatic checks off in Settings → General.
- macOS `.dmg`, the Windows `-setup.exe` (per-user) installer, the Linux AppImage and
  `.deb`/`.rpm` installs update in place. Where Aura cannot replace itself (a Windows
  `.msi` or all-users install, an app run from the disk image or through a symlink), the
  banner says why and links to the download.
- While an update installs, `aura` and the daemon refuse to start, so nothing holds the
  files being replaced. A failed or cancelled install starts the daemon again and says
  which version you have.
- The app keeps its own log, `aura-desktop.log`, next to `aurad.log` in the data
  directory, rotated at 5 files of 5 MB.
- Every window follows the daemon when it restarts, without a reload.

### Workspaces

- Keep separate lines of work apart. The name at the top of the sidebar switches
  workspaces (`⌘⇧K`, or `⌃1…9`; `Alt+Shift+1…9` on Windows and Linux); `⋯` renames,
  deletes (its sessions move to another workspace, nothing is deleted) or adds one.
  `Ctrl+Space s v` moves the selected session, with its workers, to another workspace.
- The sidebar shows leaders only: orchestrators and feature owners, each sectioned by
  its whole tree, saying for example "4 workers · 1 needs you". Leaders start collapsed;
  a worker that waits on you still shows. `⌘1…9`, `⌘[` `⌘]` and `j`/`k` move between
  leaders.
- A status rail under the list: **Elsewhere** shows what needs you and what is idle in
  the other workspaces, and **Scheduled runs** shows the runs of scheduled tasks from
  every workspace.
- **Clean up worker sessions…** (`Ctrl+Space k c`) lists the workers agents started,
  with finished ones already checked, and deletes them after one confirmation.
  Orchestrators, worktrees and branches are kept.

### Themes, and mods without a build step

- **Themes**: a JSON file in `~/.aura/themes/` that starts from the dark or light theme
  and overrides its design tokens (colours, fonts, spacing, corners), with an optional
  CSS file. Pick one in Settings → General → Theme; saved changes apply at once.
- **Mods without a build**: a mod can be plain JavaScript files in `~/.aura/mods/<id>`.
  Aura picks it up while it runs and reloads it when you save a file. It reloads only
  the half that changed.
- **For agents**: `aura guide mods` and `aura guide themes` print complete guides, and
  `aura mods new` / `aura themes new` scaffold a working mod or theme. New commands:
  `aura mods list|new|approve|enable|disable|reload|rescan|logs|call` and
  `aura themes list|new|check|use`.
- **Approvals only from your click.** A mod, or a theme's CSS, runs only after you
  approve it in a protected prompt that no theme or mod can restyle or hide. An agent
  running `aura mods approve` can only ask the app to show that prompt. Only the
  Aura window can grant an approval: the app hands the daemon it starts a secret, and
  the window proves itself with it.

### PR status

- A new bundled mod, **PR status**, shows a card in the Inspector for each pull request
  the selected session and its workers work on: state, who reviewed and who still has to,
  checks, and whether it is ready to merge, kept up to date. Approved PRs can be
  squash-merged from the card.
- A PR's title opens its page in a tab, laid out like GitHub's: conversation, commits,
  checks and changed files.
- It needs the GitHub CLI (`gh`) signed in, and works without the other mods.
- For mod authors: `ctx.openView(id, props, { preview: false })` opens a view in a tab
  of its own that stays, and `ctx.sessions.open()` / `onOpenChange()` list the sessions
  open in the window.
